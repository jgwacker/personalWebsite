We study the theory and signatures of a Mirror Twin Higgs (MTH) scenario in which stable twin neutrons constitute most of the dark matter (DM), while a subdominant component of twin elements survives as nuclear atomic dark matter (naDM). This framework offers a solution to the $matter~coincidence~problem$ ($Ω_{\rm DM}\simeq5Ω_b$), as the twin $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry can correlate the visible and DM mass scales and their number densities. We analyze twin nucleon freeze-out and the onset of twin Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN) to estimate the total naDM abundance, and determine which elements in the dark periodic table could be populated. An appreciable but subdominant naDM component generally requires a fractional nucleon mass splitting near the percent level, with twin-BBN creating heavy elements up to twin carbon. Strong twin neutron-proton scattering allows nearly all of the dark matter, not only the atomic sub-component, to participate in $dark~acoustic~oscillations$ (DAO) at early times, giving rise to a characteristic two-stage DAO signature that may be detected with future cosmological probes. Interactions between the twin and visible sectors, including the irreducible Higgs portal, make this scenario a well-motivated target for future directional direct-detection searches, and the naDM sub-component is expected to exhibit highly exotic astrophysics. We outline a supersymmetric twin Froggatt-Nielsen realization in which spontaneous $\mathbb{Z}_2$ breaking in the flavon sector can generate the required fermion spectrum and Higgs potential.

The matter coincidence, $Ω_{\rm DM}\simeq5Ω_b$, suggests a common origin for visible and dark matter (DM). We show that this motivates the study of nuclear atomic DM (naDM), which is composed of $\sim5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ mirror-neutron DM and a subdominant population of mirror elements. We realize naDM in the Mirror Twin Higgs, employing a twin Froggatt--Nielsen mechanism to generate fermion masses and realize spontaneous $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry breaking. However, the low-energy mirror framework is more general and can emerge from other ultraviolet completions. naDM gives rise to a novel two-stage dark acoustic oscillation signature, multiple correlated direct-detection signals, and highly exotic twin astrophysics.

We consider the possibility that electroweak naturalness may be epoch dependent. In no-scale supergravity, a rolling quintessence modulus can control the supersymmetry-breaking scale, allowing superpartners to be much lighter in the early universe while remaining heavy today. Fifth-force and varying-constant effects can be suppressed via correlated modular weights in the visible-sector Kähler metric and modulus-dependent superpotential Yukawa couplings, while radiative stability of the quintessence potential requires additional tuning of the field-dependent vacuum energy. The resulting effective theory has distinctive implications for early-universe phase transitions, relic abundances, and gravitational waves.

Ideas from the Swampland program (dS conjecture and TCC) suggest that it is impossible to obtain solutions of string theory/M-theory in the weak-coupling regions describing our current universe, with $V>0$, $\|\nabla V\|/V < 2/\sqrt{d-2}$ and $V \ll m^2$ (scale-separated "quasi-dS") where $m$ is the mass of the light tower. We test this expectation in M-theory compactifications and find that even after including the quantum effect of Casimir energy, for hyperbolic and (rigid) Riemann-flat manifolds, de Sitter critical points under EFT control are excluded: the slope of the potential cannot drop below the asymptotic value predicted by the TCC $\|\nabla V\|/V\gtrsim 2/\sqrt{d-2}$ except perhaps in an order-one region in the bulk of moduli space, where EFT control is lost. At the parametric level, our results hold at one-loop level in supergravity; using the scalar-field Casimir energy as a proxy, we also obtain explicit numerical bounds to provide a ballpark estimate of how much control one can expect to achieve in such settings.

A Phase-Transition Portal to Enhanced Dark Matter Annihilation

Huang, Peisi, Medina, Anibal D., Wagner, Carlos E.M., Zhang, Kairui

2611.08680 · hep-ph

Wagner; thermal DM model with phase transition, enhanced indirect detection

We realize this mechanism in a two-Higgs-doublet model extended by a…

We present a thermal dark matter (DM) scenario in which a phase transition after freeze-out changes both the DM spectrum and the dominant annihilation process, allowing an enhanced present day annihilation rate with suppressed direct detection. We realize this mechanism in a two-Higgs-doublet model extended by a complex scalar singlet, a Majorana fermion, and a heavier Dirac fermion. Before the transition, the Majorana state freezes out through $p$-wave annihilation into singlet scalars. The subsequent transition changes the dark fermion masses and mixing, enabling $s$-wave annihilation through a pseudoscalar portal. The surviving relic differs in mass and composition from the original state. We find relic compatible solutions with present day annihilation rates more than an order of magnitude above the canonical thermal value, without tuning to a mediator resonance, while satisfying direct detection bounds.

Because of the fermion doubling problem, it has been a longstanding challenge to describe a lattice algorithm for studying four-dimensional gauge theories coupled to chiral fermions. Recently, an algorithm to do so has been described by Craig, extending partial results in the previous literature. Here we describe a variant of this algorithm, based on interpolating a lattice gauge field over the continuum (as in Craig's work) and coupling the interpolated gauge field to continuum chiral fermions. The same variant was also proposed recently by Hsu. In any dimension and for any anomaly-free representation, this approach can be implemented on a computer in principle, but not in practice by Monte Carlo methods because the chiral fermion path integral is not positive.

In traditional approach to the doublet-triplet splitting problem in grand unified theories (GUTs) it is assumed that color-triplet partner of the Higgs doublet must be superheavy in order not to mediate proton decay. However, it has been proposed [1] that the splitting, instead of the mass sector, can occur among the Yukawa couplings. In this scenario the color-triplet emerges as a long-lived $T$-particle with the mass around the TeV scale. This framework provides a direct low-energy window into GUT physics. In the present paper, we study an explicit supersymmetric $SO(10)$ GUT which realizes this mechanism, including effects of gravity-mediated supersymmetry breaking. We show how the shift of the heavy fields, triggered by supersymmetry-breaking, generates a tiny coupling between the $T$-particle and the Standard Model fields and explore the resulting phenomenology. Depending on the order of gravitino mass in which the coupling emerges, the lifetime of the $T$-particle is either around a second or exceeds the age of the Universe. In our analysis, we validate some earlier constraints on (super)gravity vacua imposed by the $S$-matrix: based on the Goldstino Argument, we show that none of the GUT vacua have positive energy densities, whereas the Minkowski vacua are safe against tunneling. We also highlight the role of the species scale cutoff in sensitivity of the low energy observables towards the GUT physics.

We argue that the squark simplified model commonly used in collider analyses fails to represent realistic low-energy supersymmetric models and significantly underestimates squark production rates at hadron colliders. This has important implications for extracting squark mass limits at the LHC and assessing the potential reach of HL-LHC or the FCC-hh. Chaos on the mass shell

2611.03787 · hep-th

Using the framework of detector matching, we argue that distributions of produced particles satisfy universal evolution equations with energy,…

We study particle production in high-energy states for theories given by an RG flow between an asymptotically free or conformal theory in the UV and a massive theory in the IR. Using the framework of detector matching, we argue that distributions of produced particles satisfy universal evolution equations with energy, determined by the spectrum of light-ray operators of the UV theory. In particular, we interpret the UV Chew-Frautschi plot as a wave equation for particle densities on the mass shell, evolving with energy. (When the UV theory is a CFT, this wave equation is the same one that controls the spread of chaos in hyperbolic space at a particular temperature.) We test these ideas for integrable flows in 2d CFTs, finding an independent derivation of the evolution equations in terms of the thermodynamics of a Feynman gas. We also discuss consequences for 4d gauge theories, including the presence of a BFKL contrail produced by an outgoing wave of hadron number density.

Dark matter alters dwarf galaxy sizes and morphologies

Although the observed diversity in dwarf galaxy sizes and morphologies suggests an interplay between dark matter and baryonic physics, this relationship has…

Dwarf galaxies are the most dark-matter-dominated systems in the Universe, serving as unique laboratories for exploring the fundamental nature of the dark sector. Although the observed diversity in dwarf galaxy sizes and morphologies suggests an interplay between dark matter and baryonic physics, this relationship has remained largely unquantified in existing models. Here, we utilize a set of novel cosmological simulations with baryons to demonstrate how dark matter physics directly drives the structural evolution of these galaxies. By comparing the same systems across cold dark matter and varied self-interacting dark matter models, we find that the evolution of the central density of dark matter significantly impacts a galaxy's half-light radius, but the signal is dependent on morphology. This causal link establishes galaxy size as a direct signature of the underlying dark matter microphysics. Our findings provide a predictive framework for decoding dark matter particle properties from galactic shapes, offering a roadmap for upcoming wide-field surveys, such as the Legacy Survey of Space and Time, to probe the dark sector through the statistical analysis of galaxy populations.

Primordial or Post-Inflationary: Distinguishing the Origin of Scale-Dependent Features in the CMB

Braglia, Matteo, Qin, Wenzer, Chen, Xingang, Slatyer, Tracy R.

Slatyer co-author: distinguishing primordial from post-inflationary CMB features; inflation and early universe.

Physics beyond the Standard Model, whether of primordial or post-inflationary origin, can leave scale-dependent signals in the anisotropies of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB). In this work, we study how distinguishable these broad classes of signals are and quantify their level of degeneracy. In this initial…

Physics beyond the Standard Model, whether of primordial or post-inflationary origin, can leave scale-dependent signals in the anisotropies of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB). In this work, we study how distinguishable these broad classes of signals are and quantify their level of degeneracy. In this initial investigation, for primordial features, we limit ourselves to those of the ``sharp'' and ``resonant'' kinds; for post-inflationary features, we consider two qualitatively distinct models: exotic energy injection, which deposits energy into the primordial plasma and modifies the global thermodynamic history, and early dark energy (EDE), which affects the CMB through the background expansion and gravitational perturbations. By studying representative examples, we find that while primordial features can leave oscillatory signatures in the CMB power spectrum with essentially arbitrary frequency and running, the post-inflationary models considered here produce qualitatively different patterns that cannot generically reproduce the characteristic running of primordial feature oscillations. We verify these findings first with a principal component analysis, and show using only the most significant principal components that there are no significant degeneracies between the primordial and post-inflationary features considered here, and secondly with a full likelihood analysis, which shows that the resulting posteriors do not change significantly when considering individual classes of models or combinations of primordial/post-inflationary features. While the two post-inflationary models probe distinct physical mechanisms and both are implemented in a simplified, phenomenological fashion, the methods used in this work can be generalized to include more realistic signals or other classes of signals.

In this paper, we explore the recent observation that maximizing flavor entanglement in Higgs boson scattering in a two Higgs doublet model leads to parameter space where the potential, if bounded from below, has an enhanced mirror symmetry $U(2)^2$. Looking at $N$ Higgs doublet models, we find a generalization of the mirror models, with $U(2)^N$ symmetry. Focusing on three Higgs doublet models, we determine that there is also a bounded potential solution that maximally entangles but has only the minimal $U(2)$ symmetry. This counterexample demonstrates that additional constraints must be imposed to maintain the connection between maximal entanglement and extended symmetries. As a potential constraint, for the three Higgs doublet model, we show that requiring maximal entanglement still occurs after gauging the symmetries eliminates the counterexample, thus restoring the connection.

The function $A(k)$ appearing in the large $N$ expansion of free energy of ABJM theory on $S^3$ is known exactly from localization. An earlier calculation of the 1-loop partition function of 11d supergravity on $\mathrm{AdS}_4\times S^7/\mathbb Z_k$, based on the standard KK spectrum on $S^7$, did not reproduce it. We identify a missing 1-loop supergravity contribution as that of the 3-form field fluctuations built from square-integrable harmonic 2-forms on Euclidean $\mathrm{AdS}_4$ tensored with $S^7$ modes, which are not captured by the KK spectrum. Adding it to the KK tower contribution expressed in terms of superconformal characters and applying the $\mathbb{Z}_k$ projection, the total expression simplifies, and its evaluation in a zeta-function prescription reproduces the expression for $A(k)$. We also apply a similar approach to the 3d ADHM theory whose M-theory dual is the orbifold $\mathrm{AdS}_4\times S^7/\mathbb{Z}_p$, which has singularity along the fixed locus $\mathrm{AdS}_4\times S^3$, supporting a 7d $SU(p)$ vector multiplet. Adding the localized KK modes of this multiplet to the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-projected supergravity spectrum gives a 1-loop free energy that reproduces the ADHM localization counterpart of $A(k)$.

We investigate how the late-time decays of unstable particles into dark matter and neutrinos can leave measurable imprints on both cosmological observables and collider experiments. We study this mechanism within the scotogenic model, where the lightest singlet fermion $N_1$ serves as the dark matter candidate and is produced via the late decay of the lightest inert doublet scalar via the superWIMP mechanism. By incorporating a kination-dominated era in the early Universe, the altered expansion rate allows to match the observed $N_1$ relic density in parameter regions that would be underabundant in standard cosmology. The energy exchange between the injected neutrinos and photons resulting from the late-time decay can produce CMB spectral $μ$-distortions, detectable shifts in the effective number of relativistic species $N_{\rm eff}$, or both simultaneously. Concentrating on the lightest component of inert doublet scalars with masses between $200$ and $500~\text{GeV}$, we find that these cosmological signals are accompanied by complementary signatures accessible at the LHC. Although our analysis is derived within the scotogenic framework, our findings apply more broadly to scenarios in which the unstable particle undergoes late-time out-of-equilibrium decay to produce dark matter and non-thermal neutrinos, offering how future CMB measurements and collider searches can jointly probe dark matter production and the early Universe's thermal history.
