The BSM Rollercoaster, 1980-2000

Dimopoulos, Savas

2611.25060 · hep-ph · hep-ex · hep-th · physics.hist-ph

Dimopoulos co-author; personal retrospective on naturalness, SUSY, extra dimensions, split SUSY — core interests.

This is a personal historical retrospective, not a research result. It claims that BSM optimism rose and fell twice, in the 1980s on naturalness, technicolor and proton decay, and in the 1990s on LEP/SLC unification, with each peak ending in disappointment. The evidence is the author's own participant testimony and a deliberately subjective "confidence" curve, which is fine for the genre and not testable. The value is the inside colour on why ideas felt compelling. The closing push for Split Supersymmetry and anthropic reasoning is advocacy resting on opinion, not new argument.

Abstract

This contribution is a personal historical retrospective on beyond-the-Standard-Model (BSM) physics from 1978 to the early 2000s, organized around its emotional rollercoaster. I trace two waves of optimism separated by a multiyear contraction of confidence, both ultimately ending in disappointment when decisive experimental confirmation failed to arrive. The story begins in 1978, when the Standard Model emerged as the front-runner. It then moves from the first wave of excitement over naturalness and Technicolor, rumored proton-decay events, and the construction of the Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model, to the contraction of confidence in the late 1980s. A dramatic revival came in 1991, when the LEP and SLC precision data pointed toward supersymmetric unification, but it eventually gave way to renewed disappointment over the absence of superpartners and the sharpening of the LEP paradox. I also describe how the realization that Newtonian gravity had been tested only down to distances of order centimeters opened the door to large extra dimensions. Finally, I discuss how Weinberg's 1987 anthropic argument for the cosmological constant, the 1998 discovery of cosmic acceleration, and the emergence of the string landscape changed the intellectual status of naturalness itself and led to Split Supersymmetry. I have tried to describe not only what was proposed, but why these ideas felt compelling at the time, why confidence rose and fell, and how later evidence changed our sense of what could count as an explanation.

A Positive Metric on Four-Dimensional QFTs

Hartman, Thomas, Mathys, Grégoire, Stergiou, Andreas

2611.23370 · hep-th

Hartman: ANEC implies strong a-theorem, positive metric on QFT space; big-name formal hep-th.

The paper claims a non-perturbative generalization of the Jack-Osborn metric on the space of 4D QFTs, and shows it is non-negative by the averaged null energy condition, so ANEC implies the strong a-theorem. The route is spectral decomposition of ⟨O_I E O_J⟩ under null rotations that fix the light ray, giving positivity at each impact parameter. If it holds, this is a real advance: it supplies a monotonic function along the flow, which goes beyond the Komargodski-Schwimmer dilaton argument. I can't verify the details from the excerpt, so I'd check the non-perturbative definition and the relation to the existing ANEC-based a-theorem proofs.

Abstract

A natural metric on the space of four-dimensional quantum field theories was introduced by Jack and Osborn in classic work on the local renormalization group. In perturbation theory, the Jack-Osborn metric determines the flow of the $a$-anomaly, and positivity implies the $a$-theorem. We derive a non-perturbative generalization of the Jack-Osborn metric and show that it is non-negative by the averaged null energy condition (ANEC). Consequently, the ANEC implies the strong $a$-theorem, and provides the monotonic function along the flow. The method is based on spectral analysis of the three-point function $\langle \mathcal{O}_I \mathcal{E} \mathcal{O}_J\rangle$, where the $\mathcal{O}_I$ are relevant operators and $\mathcal{E}$ is the averaged null energy. This three-point function is decomposed under the spectrum of null rotations that fix the light ray, leading to a positivity condition at each impact parameter.

Feebly-Interacting Particle Searches

Feng, Jonathan L.

2611.21719 · hep-ph · hep-ex

Feng review of FIP motivations, dark matter and quirks; adjacent and by a big name.

This is a conference review (ICHEP 2026) arguing that feebly-interacting particles are now mainstream, resting on three motivations: FIPs are one of only two frontier options, thermal freezeout gives "WIMPless miracle" targets along the whole mass-coupling diagonal, and portal couplings are non-decoupling and tied to the strong CP problem, neutrino mass and EWSB. Nothing here is new, and the evidence is a schematic mass-coupling plot and parametric scaling rather than any calculation or fresh result. The "only two possibilities" framing is oversold, since it ignores heavy weakly-coupled states and the light-but-feeble corner is where much of the real model-building lives. The promised survey of ALPs, X17, quirks and the Forward Physics Facility is thin.

Abstract

In the last several years, feebly-interacting particles (FIPs) have moved from "exotica" to the mainstream of particle physics. This development is fueled by three motivations: FIPs are one of two broad possibilities for new particles, FIPs are as well motivated by dark matter as WIMPs, and FIPs are connected to important problems in particle physics. I review these motivations and then briefly highlight recent progress in a few areas, including axion-like particles, dark photons and other gauge bosons, the X17 anomaly, quirks, and the Forward Physics Facility.

Geometric Quantization of the Giant Graviton Master Moduli Space

Budzik, Kasia, Gaiotto, Davide

2611.18539 · hep-th · math-ph · math.MP

Gaiotto on giant gravitons and holography; formal hep-th with a big name.

The paper claims finite-N BPS operators of free or twisted U(N) gauge theories can be reorganized as closed-string Fock space plus geometrically quantized Giant Graviton moduli space, modulo "open loop equations" implemented by a BRST complex. The evidence is thin: the central statement is a conjecture, checked "experimentally" in examples where the cohomology map is not always injective, and it only matches Murthy's Giant Graviton Expansion at the level of characters. The categorification of that expansion is a neat algebraic step, but it is formal, BPS-only and free-theory. The black-hole microstate claims are speculation, and no interacting or non-supersymmetric check is given.

Abstract

We describe a remarkable reorganization of the space of gauge invariant operators of free or twisted $U(N)$ gauge theories as combinations of single-trace and determinant operators modulo certain "open loop equations". The reorganization has a transparent holographic interpretation, describing how states at finite $N$ can be reproduced by imposing certain $N$-independent gauge constraints on the combination of the closed strings Fock space and a geometric quantization of the moduli space of Giant Graviton D-branes, with quantization parameter $N^{-1}$. The moduli space of Giant Graviton D-branes is reproduced from tachyon condensation of auxiliary D-brane/anti-D-brane pairs which are "Koszul dual" to the D-brane system which supports the original gauge theory. We describe how to impose the open loop equations via certain BRST complexes and conjecture that their homology recovers the finite $N$ BPS Hilbert space in the limit of large auxiliary rank. The BRST construction conjecturally categorifies Murthy's Giant Graviton Expansion of indices counting $U(N)$-invariant BPS operators.

The Full-Shape Analysis of the Fading Dark Sector Model

Obied, Georges, Vafa, Cumrun, Wu, David H.

2611.17422 · astro-ph.CO · hep-ph · hep-th

Vafa string-motivated fading dark sector fit to DESI; adjacent cosmology with strong author signal.

The paper adds DESI DR1 full-shape data to the Fading Dark Sector model, a string-motivated scalar coupling dark energy and dark matter. It reports Δχ²≈-4 against CPL and 4.1σ over ΛCDM, with (c,c') unchanged. A 4 χ² gain at equal parameter count is modest, and the 4.1σ mostly comes from the supernova and BAO data already used before. The "only model with this significance" claim is oversold. The coupled-sector EFTofLSS is unvalidated against N-body runs, and the ~0.5% fifth force conflicts with the disk-warp bound, which the paper waves away.

Abstract

We analyze the Fading Dark Sector (FDS) model, which is motivated by string theory and in which the fading of the dark sector is driven by a scalar field. The model is specified by two $O(1)$ numbers $(c,c')$ that parametrize the logarithmic derivatives of the dark energy and dark matter densities with respect to the field in Planck units. In previous work, we showed that this model provides a good fit to the DESI BAO, CMB, and SN datasets. Here, we extend that analysis to include the DESI DR1 full-shape galaxy power spectrum, which adds information about the time evolution of cosmic structure and provides an independent consistency check of the FDS model. We find that, when the DESI DR1 full-shape data are combined with CMB+DESI DR2 BAO+DES-Dovekie, the FDS model outperforms the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) $w_0$-$w_a$ parameterization by $Δχ^2_{\rm MAP}=-4$ at equal parameter count and is preferred over $Λ$CDM at $4.1σ$. The inferred values of $(c,c')$ with the full-shape data are unchanged from our previous analysis, so this improvement indicates that FDS makes a prediction for the growth of structure that captures the observed growth history better than the $w_0$-$w_a$ parameterization.

Microcausality Bounds on Dispersion Laws

Creminelli, Paolo, Mancini, Michael, McBlain, Warin Patrick

2611.17407 · hep-th · astro-ph.CO · gr-qc

Creminelli on microcausality bounds, with implications for the EFT of inflation.

The paper shows that microcausality (Im ω ≤ |Im k|) makes k ± ω(k) typically real functions, which gives two-sided bounds on all Taylor coefficients of a linear Goldstone dispersion relation in units of its radius of convergence R. The bounds shrink linearly in 1−c_s, so higher-derivative quadratic operators must vanish as c_s→1, including in the EFT of Inflation. The math is clean and optimal for ω(k), but it extends Heller et al. with geometric function theory, and it is tree-level and non-dissipative. The ω² bounds are only necessary, not sufficient, and loops and interactions, where the physics would matter, are left open.

Abstract

Microcausality holds in any state, including those that spontaneously break Lorentz invariance, and requires the poles (and other singularities) of the retarded Green's function to satisfy $\mathrm{Im}\,ω\le |\mathrm{Im}\,k|$ throughout the complex plane. Building on the work of Heller, Serantes, Spaliński and Withers, we study its consequences for dispersion relations that are linear at small $k$, like Goldstone bosons, assuming tree-level, non-dissipative dynamics. We show that microcausality is equivalent to requiring that $k \pm ω(k)$ be typically real functions. This yields two-sided bounds on all higher-order coefficients of $ω(k)$ in units of its radius of convergence $R$, the scale at which the linear Goldstone description breaks down. The optimal constraints are characterised by an infinite hierarchy of Toeplitz determinants. We derive analogous bounds on $ω^2(k)$, whose radius of convergence $\tilde R$ marks the scale at which new states must appear. The bounds shrink to zero as the sound speed $c_s$ approaches 1: for a fixed higher-derivative correction, both $R$ and $\tilde R$ become parametrically smaller than naive dimensional analysis suggests. In particular, in the EFT of Inflation all higher-derivative quadratic operators must vanish as $c_s \to 1$. We test the bounds in several models: a $U(1)$ superfluid, light in a dielectric medium, photon propagation in a strong magnetic field, colour-flavour-locked quark matter, and a homogeneous solid. Finally, we extend the bounds to gapped modes.
