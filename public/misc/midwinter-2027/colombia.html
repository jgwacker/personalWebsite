Cartagena and Medellín

February is Cartagena's best month: dry, breezy, and lit from six in the morning. The walled city and Getsemaní are the best evening street-photography location on this list, Bazurto market is the rawest, and a 75-minute hop to Medellín adds cable cars over the hillside barrios. The costs are heat that stops work from ten to four, a one-stop flight each way, no legal drone, and hotels where 'suite' rarely means two rooms.

The photographs

In order of priority

  1. The walled city before 06:30: Torre del Reloj, Plaza de los Coches, San Diego's balconies and bougainvillea; palenqueras set up around eight (pay for portraits, agree first). Baluarte de Santo Domingo at dawn beats Café del Mar at sunset.
  2. Getsemaní: Calle de la Sierpe and Calle San Juan under umbrellas and flags, murals, Plaza de la Trinidad from six in the evening.
  3. Bazurto market, 7 to 9 with a local guide: superb colour in produce, filthy in the fish halls, phone-snatch territory.
  4. Medellín's metrocable and Comuna 13's escalators and murals, with a guide, before ten.
  5. If Bogotá replaces Medellín: Paloquemao flower aisles at five in the morning, La Candelaria's doors, Monserrate at dusk.

Eating vegetarian

Cities are fine; the coast is fish-and-meat heavy and the traps are stock and pork fat rather than visible meat. Cooks sometimes count chicken stock as vegetarian, so ask about the broth.

Order. Arepa de queso and arepa de choclo, patacones (ask if fried in vegetable oil; usually yes), arroz con coco, huevos pericos, changua in Bogotá, mote de queso confirmed sin carne, sopa de verduras, pandebono, almojábana, buñuelos, hogao on anything, avocado, and the fruit: corozo, níspero, zapote, lulo, mango biche with salt and lime. Girasoles and Demente in Cartagena; plenty in Medellín's Laureles.

Traps. Caldo and most sopa del día (beef-rib stock), ajiaco, sancocho, frijoles paisas and lentils cooked with pork, default empanadas, arepa de huevo and carimañola (ground beef inside), tamales, anything from a shared grill.

Drone and tripod

A customs resolution in force since January 2026 treats drones as controlled imports entering only through Bogotá airport or the Cartagena port with an advance declaration and tax; non-compliant drones are seized. Registration, a 120 m ceiling, daylight only and airport exclusion zones follow, and Cartagena's airport is 3 km from the walled city. Leave it home. Tripods need no permit on public streets; the walled city is too crowded after eight, Bazurto is a no, the walls and Castillo San Felipe tolerate them.

Where to stay

Bands from listings, not live quotes; pricing is the next step

Walled-city and Getsemaní hotels are small colonial houses, often couples-oriented; confirm floor plans before trusting the word suite. Top band: Casa San Agustín junior suites about US$550 to 600, Casa Pestagua from about US$575, Sofitel Santa Clara with four-night minimums at peak. Family-capable mid band: Bantú (connecting rooms, rooftop pool), Casa La Fe, Casa del Coliseo, Casa La Cartujita, Ananda, US$250 to 450. Medellín suites in Poblado or Laureles US$150 to 300.

Risks

  • Heat: work from six to nine and four to seven, pool between.
  • Cruise-day crowds and Valentine's pricing in the walled city.
  • US State Department Level 3 since March 2026; the Level 4 zones are far from this route, and the July 2026 embassy alert on dating-app druggings is not a family risk, but phone snatching in Getsemaní at night and Bazurto is.
  • The Houston to Cartagena nonstop is new; verify it is still flying before booking.
  • The vegetarian eats a lot of cheese arepas on the coast.
