The six, ranked

Rank is my read against the brief; the pages have the evidence

1

Taiwan at the Lantern Festival

Lantern Festival on the last night

Strong candidate. The festival timing is a gift, the food is the easiest of the six for a vegetarian, and no leg is more than two hours. Taipei weather and the unannounced Pingxi date are the two things to watch.

2

Mexico City and Oaxaca

Markets every day, no jet lag

The least friction of the six and the most time on the ground. Weak points: no festival, a tripod ban inside archaeological sites, monarch butterflies only if a 2.5-hour drive is allowed, and Oaxacan lard and chicken stock for the vegetarian.

3

Hokkaido in Winter

Cranes in river steam, drift ice, a new nonstop

The highest photographic ceiling of the six and the hardest trip: three internal legs, dawns at minus fifteen to minus twenty, a crane village with no suites, and the hardest country here for a vegetarian. Worth it if the cranes are the point.

4

Cartagena and Medellín

Colour and street life, dry-season Caribbean

Strong for the photographer, fine for the vegetarian in the cities, no jet lag to speak of. Heat, cruise-ship crowds and the Level 3 advisory are the real negatives; Barranquilla's Carnaval is missed by four days.

5

Delhi, Agra and Jaipur

Ideal season, six days on the ground

Doable but tight, and the best vegetarian and bazaar photography of the six. Delhi haze, no tripods at the Taj, two six-o'clock trains with a tired child, and stomach illness as the likeliest trip-wrecker. A better fit for a longer break.

6

Antigua and Lake Atitlán

Highland markets, volcanoes, one bad road

Marginal on the drive rule, no nonstop in February, a Level 3 advisory. It passes only if two three-hour car days with a lunch stop are acceptable. Otherwise the one-base alternative is Antigua alone, which is a smaller trip.

How I would choose

Two of the six fit the brief with almost nothing to argue about. Taiwan has the only festival that lands inside the window, on the last night, the easiest vegetarian food in Asia, high-speed rail for every move, and night markets and temples that are exactly the street-life brief; its costs are a 16-hour time shift, seven nights instead of eight because of the dateline, and a Taipei sky that is grey more days than not. Mexico City and Oaxaca is the opposite trade: a four-and-a-half-hour flight, no jet lag, all eight nights on the ground in dry sun, and a different village market every weekday, but no festival and a tripod ban inside the ruins.

Hokkaido has the highest ceiling of the six, cranes dancing in river steam at minus fifteen, and the hardest week: three internal flights and trains, no suites in the crane village, dawns that will test Zoe, and a country where the vegetarian has to interrogate every bowl. It is the trip to take if the cranes are the point. Cartagena is the best evening street photography on the list and the one most likely to be too hot and too crowded. North India loses two of its nine days to the flights and would be a better trip over a longer break. Guatemala fails the two-hour drive rule unless you accept two three-hour car days.

The next step is live fares for the top two or three on the exact dates, then hotels and a day-by-day page for the one you pick.

Considered and set aside

  • Egypt. Cairo, Luxor and Aswan are perfect in February, but it would be the third Islamic-heritage trip in five months after Morocco and Uzbekistan, and the flights alone take two of the nine days.
  • Vietnam and Cambodia. Hanoi and Angkor are dry and cool now, but 17 hours of flying each way leaves five days on the ground.
  • Iceland and Lofoten. Ice caves and northern lights peak in February, but both depend on long winter drives or tour buses.
  • Cuba. Havana is the photographer's obvious pick; US travel rules make it the complicated one.
  • Honshu, Japan. Tokyo, the snow monkeys, Takayama and Kanazawa are bombproof by rail, but every snow festival ends before Feb 13. Kept as the fallback inside the Japan page.
