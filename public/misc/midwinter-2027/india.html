Delhi, Agra and Jaipur

February is the month to see North India: dry, 25 degrees, cool dawns, and Old Delhi lit up every evening for Ramzan iftar. The vegetarian eats better here than anywhere else on this list. The problem is arithmetic: Air India's nonstop lands at one in the morning on Monday, and the return leaves Sunday morning, so the nine-day window buys six real days, enough for the Delhi, Agra, Jaipur triangle by train and nothing more.

Verdict. Doable but tight, and the best vegetarian and bazaar photography of the six. Delhi haze, no tripods at the Taj, two six-o'clock trains with a tired child, and stomach illness as the likeliest trip-wrecker. A better fit for a longer break.

The photographs

In order of priority

  1. Old Delhi: Jama Masjid's steps at dawn, Khari Baoli spice market, iftar evenings in Matia Mahal.
  2. The Taj at gate opening, then Mehtab Bagh at sunset, where tripods are allowed.
  3. Jaipur before eight: an empty street at Hawa Mahal, Phool Mandi's flowers, bazaars opening, Amber at its opening.
  4. Agra's Kinari Bazar lanes and bedai breakfast stalls.
  5. If Varanasi replaces Jaipur: a dawn boat on Magh Purnima, Feb 20.

Air

Delhi's February average sits around 220 to 240 on the Indian index, 'unhealthy' on the US scale, worst at dawn. Agra fog fades by mid-month; Jaipur is cleaner. N95s for Zoe in Delhi.

Eating vegetarian

The easiest country on the list for a vegetarian; the discipline is water and raw food, not menus.

Order. Delhi: chole bhature, the parathas of Paranthe Wali Gali, winter-only daulat ki chaat. Agra: bedai with aloo, petha. Rajasthan: dal baati churma, gatte ki sabzi, ker sangri, pyaaz kachori, Lassiwala's lassi.

Traps. Old Delhi's Ramzan street food is mostly meat; go for the pictures. Bottled or filtered water only, no unknown ice, no raw salads, hot cooked food; travellers' diarrhoea runs above 60 percent on a two-week trip, so carry rehydration salts, loperamide for the adults and a paediatric azithromycin prescription for Zoe.

Drone and tripod

Foreign visitors cannot register a drone, the import needs a licence, and the Taj, Lutyens' Delhi and every ASI monument are no-fly zones regardless. Leave it home; a licensed Indian operator is the only legal route. Tripods: the Taj's official site lists them as prohibited and bans photography inside the mausoleum; ASI sites generally refuse them. Privately run Amber and the City Palace usually allow them; streets and Mehtab Bagh are fine. Bring a beanbag.

Where to stay

Bands from listings, not live quotes; pricing is the next step

Heritage havelis give a true separate sleeping room at about US$220 to 500: Haveli Dharampura in Old Delhi, Samode Haveli in Jaipur, ITC Mughal suites in Agra. Palaces run US$700 to 1,500 and up (Rambagh, Sujan Rajmahal, Oberoi Amarvilas). E-visas, about US$25 each, through the official site only, applied for in late January; CDC calls for hepatitis A and typhoid for all three and a rabies pre-exposure discussion for Zoe because of street dogs and monkeys.

Risks

  • Six days is the floor; a flight delay removes Delhi, not Agra.
  • Air India retimes this route often; book early and re-check in January.
  • Delhi haze blunts dawn vistas; shoot street and interiors there.
  • Six o'clock trains on days four and six.
  • Stomach illness; be strict for the first three days.
