Mexico City and Oaxaca

A four-and-a-half-hour nonstop, a two-hour time change, and eight full nights on the ground in the driest, sunniest month. Oaxaca's valley has a different village market every weekday, and Mexico City adds Teotihuacan from a balloon at sunrise. Carnaval ends four days before you arrive, so there is no festival, but the weekly markets are the better photograph anyway.

Verdict. The least friction of the six and the most time on the ground. Weak points: no festival, a tripod ban inside archaeological sites, monarch butterflies only if a 2.5-hour drive is allowed, and Oaxacan lard and chicken stock for the vegetarian.

The photographs

In order of priority

  1. Teotihuacan from a balloon at sunrise, then in at the 08:00 opening. The Pyramid of the Moon has been closed since the April 2026 shooting; the balloon is the fix.
  2. Tlacolula on Sunday: tarp-covered streets, red Ocotlán pottery, barbacoa smoke, best from 9 to 11.
  3. Mercado 20 de Noviembre and Benito Juárez at 7 in the morning, and the Pasillo de Humo at night.
  4. Monte Albán at the 08:00 opening for empty plazas and low side light. No sunrise access, no tripod.
  5. Hierve el Agua's infinity pool, with Teotitlán del Valle's weavers on the way; a coin flip on closures.

Eating vegetarian

Mexico City is easy. Oaxaca is harder than it looks: unrefined pork lard (asiento or manteca) is smeared on tlayudas, memelas and tortillas and stirred into beans, and chicken stock goes into rice, salsas and all seven moles.

Order. Esquites and elotes, tejate, guacamole, squash-blossom or quesillo quesadillas asked for without asiento, eggs at a comedor, a sit-down tlayuda ordered sin asiento, sin carne, hot chocolate with pan de yema. Named: Calabacitas Tiernas (vegetarian), Chilhuacle Rojo's vegan coloradito, the Pochote market mole stall, Praga, Xiguela. In Mexico City: Por Siempre Vegana, Los Loosers, Páramo.

Traps. Street-stall black beans cooked in lard; every mole; rice cooked in chicken stock; quesillo made with animal rennet; Casa Oaxaca and Los Danzantes have little for a vegetarian beyond soup and pasta.

Drone and tripod

INAH treats tripods as professional equipment needing written authorisation ten business days ahead, applied for in person, with a daily fee reported around 10,900 pesos. Tripods are stopped at the gate at Teotihuacan, Monte Albán and Mitla; handheld is fine, and streets, markets and the Santo Domingo façade at blue hour are no problem. Drones: every INAH site is a no-fly zone with fines, and foreigners cannot complete the registration required over 250 g, so only a Mini is legal, in open country, by day.

Where to stay

Bands from listings, not live quotes; pricing is the next step

Roma Norte and Condesa one-bedroom suites (Brick, Condesa DF, La Valise, Nima Local House, Casa Goliana) run US$300 to 550; apart-hotels with a sofa bed US$120 to 250; Centro family rooms US$200 to 350 and better for dawn walks. Oaxaca Centro suites (Quinta Real's ex-convent, Casa Antonieta, Pug Seal, Sin Nombre, Grana) US$200 to 450; Marialicia Suites apartments US$150 to 200. The Feb 14 weekend fills with domestic couples.

Risks

  • Teotihuacan's pyramids may still be closed to climbing.
  • Hierve el Agua closes unpredictably over a land dispute; Mitla, Yagul and Teotitlán are the fallback day.
  • Monarchs need a decision on a 2.5-hour drive.
  • No tripod inside sites; no real drone.
  • Mexico City immigration can take 45 to 90 minutes on arrival; Aeromexico is the reliable carrier to Oaxaca, Volaris and Viva fly only about five times a week.
