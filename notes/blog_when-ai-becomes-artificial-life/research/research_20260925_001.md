## Prior art to cite / engage (ranked)
1. **Hendrycks, "Natural Selection Favors AIs over Humans"** (arXiv:2303.16200, Mar 2023) and **"An Overview of Catastrophic AI Risks"** AI-race section (arXiv:2306.12001, Jun 2023). THE direct predecessor: applies Lewontin's three conditions to competing AI; "selfish behaviors do not require malicious intent"; AIs may "not even be aware of what a human is but still behave selfishly"; safety constraints are competitively disadvantaged ("never break the law" loses to "don't get caught"). CAVEAT: their selective environment is competition among developers/corporations/militaries + markets, NOT a pure population of self-replicating artifacts.
2. **Ben Turtel, "Darwin's LLMs"** (bturtel.substack.com, Nov 2025). Closest contemporary essay: applies Lewontin's three criteria to LLMs, Darwinian evolution with no intent, fitness ≠ intelligence. Kindred blog piece.
3. **Kulveit et al., "Gradual Disempowerment"** (arXiv:2501.16946, Jan 2025). Best support for "superintelligence not required" + "local alignment insufficient at system level": catastrophe from incremental competitive drift even while each system stays locally aligned. CAVEAT: socioeconomic incentive drift, not literal Darwinian reproduction; presupposes capable AI.
4. **Hammond et al., "Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI"** (arXiv:2502.14143, Feb 2025, ~50 authors incl. DeepMind). Names "selection pressures" as a core risk factor; emergent agency "cannot be ascribed to any individual." Supports the third-category framing. CAVEAT: emergent-agency section self-described as speculative; frame is multi-agent-vs-single, compatible with superintelligence.
5. **Guo et al., "Model Collapse as Cultural Evolution"** (arXiv:2605.23054, 2026) + "LLM as a Broken Telephone" (arXiv:2502.20258). For Claim 5: heredity runs through the data stream; structure survives only under quality-based selection. CAVEAT: cultural-evolution/iterated-learning framing; selection is experimenter-imposed, not autonomous; single preprint. Complementary: Shumailov et al. (Nature 2024) tail-loss; Gerstgrasser et al. 2024 (accumulation vs replacement qualifies inevitability).
6. **Motwani et al., "Secret Collusion among AI Agents"** (arXiv:2402.07510, Oxford/DeepMind, 2024) + follow-ups. For Claim 6 (covert coordination), but IN-PRINCIPLE only.

## The counterargument the post MUST engage
**Boudry & Friederich, "The selfish machine?"** Philosophical Studies (2024/2025, DOI 10.1007/s11098-024-02226-3; open at philsci-archive.pitt.edu/23903). Peer-reviewed reply to Hendrycks: AI development "resembles the domestication of animals more than the blind, Darwinian natural selection"; using Godfrey-Smith's Darwinian spaces, "the more evolution is top-down, directed and driven by intelligent agency, the less paradigmatically Darwinian it becomes"; market competition need not favor selfish traits. KEY CONCESSION the post can exploit: they grant that if AIs "go feral" (reproduce autonomously without human supervision) it "could pose grave danger" — that feral case is exactly the post's scenario. (Distinct from the Boudry reply to the PNAS 2026 piece already cited.) Related contrarian: Quillette "Why AI Is Not About to Go Feral" (2026); Tabarrok, Marginal Revolution "Does Natural Selection Favor AIs over Humans? Model This" (2023, economic critique); LessWrong "Against most, but not all, AI risk analogies" (targets analogy-based reasoning, i.e. the antibiotic analogy).
